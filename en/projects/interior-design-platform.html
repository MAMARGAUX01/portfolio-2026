Interior design project management platform

A studio, a client, months of project and scattered information: how can progress be made clear for everyone?

Role
UX researcher and designer
Tools
Figma, Notion
Type
Concept project, solo
01

Problem and context

Interior architecture projects involve many actors: designers, clients, contractors, suppliers and manufacturers. Projects can last several months and require constant coordination between all stakeholders.

During my work in an interior design studio, I observed that most project management still happens through a mix of phone calls, emails, spreadsheets and scattered documents. This creates delays, lost information and unnecessary stress for both designers and clients.

This project explores how a digital platform could centralise project communication, giving both the studio and the client a clearer view of the project at every stage.

Problem statement

How might we design a simple, transparent platform that improves designer–client communication while reducing the studio’s administrative overhead?

  • Designers spend a large portion of their time chasing information instead of designing.
  • Clients lack visibility on project progress and often feel uncertain during long projects.
  • Information gets lost between emails, calls and documents.
02

Approach and analysis

Studio observation

Designers constantly switching between phone calls, emails and spreadsheets; clients requesting updates; supplier information hard to track.

Informal interviews

Conversations about daily coordination challenges, communication with clients, supplier tracking, and pain points in current workflows.

Competitive analysis

Asana, Monday, Notion, Houzz Pro, Studio Designer: most are either too generic or not designed for direct client access.

What the research showed

  • Communication with clients consumes a large amount of time; many interactions could be simplified with direct access to updates.
  • Information is scattered across multiple tools, which increases the risk of miscommunication and delays.
  • Clients want reassurance during long projects: transparency plays a key role in building trust.
  • Designers want to focus on creativity: any solution should reduce administrative overhead, not add to it.
03

Prototypes

04

Solution: benefits and limits

Appointment system

A simple scheduling flow: clients book their first consultation without contacting the studio manually.

Project dashboard

A personalised project page with overview, moodboards, current phase and milestones.

Project timeline

A visual timeline of project stages, improving transparency and reducing uncertainty for clients.

Expected benefits

  • Project communication centralised in one place.
  • Better visibility of progress for the client.
  • Less administrative overhead for the studio.

Limits

  • Mid-fidelity prototype: the high-fidelity interface is still to be designed.
  • Testing needs to be extended to more designers.
  • Supplier management is not covered: linking deliveries to the client-facing timeline is the most complex remaining problem.
  • Mobile use on site still to be explored.

This project showed how deeply work practices influence product design: interior designers care about how their tools reflect their professional image to clients. I centred the platform on the client experience because client anxiety was the primary source of unnecessary designer workload.
